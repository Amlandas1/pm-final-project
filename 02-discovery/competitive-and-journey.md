# Competitive Analysis & Journey Map (Module 2)

## Responses
- **Role, who are you solving for? (the specific user segment or profile):** Tom (The Churned High-Value Subscriber)
Role: A lapsed, quality-conscious subscriber who cancelled his subscription after realizing that bloated content volume was actively degrading his evening leisure time.

Marcus (The Disillusioned Film Buff)
Role: A dedicated cinema enthusiast trapped in a repetitive, low-diversity algorithmic loop that treats his sophisticated taste like a generic demographic.
- **Goal, what is this user ultimately trying to achieve?:** Tom (The Churned High-Value Subscriber)
To invest his limited leisure time into guaranteed, high-caliber films that deliver immediate emotional and intellectual payoff without administrative friction.

Marcus (The Disillusioned Film Buff)
To uncover hidden gems, authentic diversity, and curated cinematic discoveries that reward a sophisticated palette.
- **Friction, the main barrier (moment of misery) stopping them from succeeding:** Tom (The Churned High-Value Subscriber)
The "Warehouse Fatigue" Churn (UXR-04) — Experiencing an environment where total title volume went up while the quality of his evenings plummeted, ultimately driving him to permanently cancel and migrate to a competitor providing hand-picked selections.

Marcus (The Disillusioned Film Buff)
The Franchise Echo Chamber (UXR-02 / BUG-1091) — Being fed three near-duplicate action sequels simply for watching one title, destroying his sense of discovery and making him yearn for human curation over algorithmic volume.
- **External tools, the outside platforms or tools the user is forced to use:** Tom outsources his discovery entirely to a competitor's human-curated email digest

Marcus bypasses StreamLine's algorithm and conducts manual off-platform research before ever opening the app such as such as friends , film critics, or external cinephile databases
- **The process, the 3 to 5 manual steps the user takes to get the job done:** Tom : Specific Steps Taken:

Ignores the StreamLine app home screen completely, treating it strictly as a passive utility rather than a discovery engine.

Opens a competitor's weekly email newsletter featuring two hand-picked film recommendations.

Commits to watching those external selections—and ultimately cancels his StreamLine subscription when he realizes he no longer relies on the platform for value.

Marcus : Specific Steps Taken:

Consults trusted human sources—such as friends (UXR-10), film critics, or external cinephile databases—to find a genuine "hidden gem" (UXR-02).

Memorizes or writes down the exact title, knowing that StreamLine's natural-language and mood-based search will fail (BUG-1080, UXR-07).

Opens StreamLine solely to type the exact string into the search bar, enduring slow cold-start latency (BUG-1110) and loud autoplay disruptions (UXR-05) just to check availability.
- **Core frustration, the exact moment the process feels most “broken”:** Both users feel abandoned by the product. Instead of acting as a trusted guide that elevates their free time, StreamLine shifts the entire burden of discovery onto the user—making high-intent viewers feel like their subscription delivers no curation value whatsoever.
